About the wave and gates

Choose N = 2, 3, 4 or 5. The box and mass stay fixed: T = Trev/(2N), and the inverse is a positive-time wait of (2N−1)T. There is one scalar wave and no spin channel.

Each box labels an orthogonal logical mode built from N sine eigenmodes along each axis, with N selectable from 2 to 5. Packets overlap, so logical-mode probability differs from probability inside its geometrical box.

Labels use binary position registers for 2×2 and 4×4, and |x,y⟩ for 3×3 and 5×5. Columns increase left to right; rows increase bottom to top. The input is the lower-left packet |0,0⟩ (|0000⟩ at 4×4).

ℋ = −ℏ²∇²/(2m)
A = U(T),   A† = U((2N−1)T),   U(2NT) = I

Preparation and forward mixing wait for T = πℏ/(NE₁). The inverse waits forward for (2N−1)T under the same positive kinetic Hamiltonian. The fixed revival time is 19.2 gate-time seconds. At the default 4×4 size, T is 2.4 and 7T is 16.8. Both axes evolve simultaneously.

Preparation gives equal probabilities with definite relative phases. The Bloch sphere uses those phases in its marked/unmarked basis. Oracle and reference pulses remain ideal mode-projector phase operations. The first optimal peak takes 1, 2, 3 or 3 iterations for 2×2, 3×3, 4×4 or 5×5 respectively.

Free mixing: j = (ℏ/m) Im(ψ*∇ψ)
Phase pulses: j = ∇χ,   ∇²χ = −∂ρ/∂t
Particle velocity: v = j / ρ

During free evolution the arrows and particles use the ordinary Bohmian current. During the ideal nonlocal phase pulses they use the chosen conserved curl-free transport. Every stage has zero normal flux at the outer walls.

Compare particle fractions with “Inside goal box.” Arrow lengths show current strength on a compressed scale. Pause and manual gate checkpoints hold the wave, current, particles, and trails still for inspection.
